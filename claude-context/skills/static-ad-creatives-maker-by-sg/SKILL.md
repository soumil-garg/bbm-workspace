# Static ad creatives maker - BY SG (v3.0)

One rule behind everything: **every creative is a hypothesis about a specific person, a specific emotion and a specific reason to buy now.** We make the best hypotheses we can, and the ad account kills the wrong ones. Scope: D2C, static images. Tone with Soumil: direct, call him sir, have an opinion, never assume on client work.

## The output standard (v3.0, 2026-10-10: the Daigo round 4 set, "I love each and every one of the creatives")
Every run delivers the same thing, built the same way. The manual is `references/whole-ad-recipe.md`; the gold example is `references/templates/ads_config.example.json` (13 prompts that rebuild byte for byte) with the pictures and briefs in `references/examples/daigo_round4/` (`gold/` holds the numbered sheets of the 15 pictures and their exact prompts).
1. **References early.** Right after Gate 1 (they are not shown to him and can be made while the copy gate runs): the real pack photo (banned badges, seals and icons removed by the model when the claims audit bans them) and a reference for every product form that appears (sachet, stick pack, jar, tube, scoop), made once per brand, saved in `C:\cca\<brand>\ref\` and reused every round.
2. **Lines first, in both languages.** A pool of candidate lines per picture, Hinglish and English for a bilingual market (about half and half in the set), scored by an independent critic AND screened by a separate native Hindi and Hinglish reader for double meanings, filtered by the claim register and the policy checklist, reconciled into a table with a Pick column, and approved by Soumil as plain text BEFORE any image is made (Gate 2).
3. **The model draws the WHOLE ad in one pass:** scene, the real pack, the branded product forms, headline, small line and badge, from the references. One prompt anatomy (reference roles + construction brief + concept + avoid list), built by `scripts/whole_ad.py` from one JSON config. No code text layer, no cut-out pack, no tile.
4. **One story per picture:** the line says the idea, the picture proves it, the product takes part in it. A poster, not a snapshot: one flat or studio colour field or a believable built set, one soft key light, one focal cluster, a headline of 2 to 7 words with a treatment (highlight bar, two-colour split, outline, stacked sizes), and a concrete number, a joke, a contrast or a question.
5. **Delivery by `scripts/deliver_ads.py`:** 4:5 pictures numbered 1..N as 1080x1350 PNGs with clean names, numbered contact sheets (up to 8 pictures each), `PROMPTS_roundN_exact.md`, the folder opened, the sheets and the prompts sent. About $0.066 a picture, a round of 13 to 15 about $1.
6. **QA before he sees anything:** by eye at full size and at zoom (`references/qa-gate.md`: the 14 checks plus the whole-ad checks A to H), then the self-check against his approved ads, then a short message: the key (number, line, language), cost, what was checked, what is not clean, your five picks, what is not done, ask for picks by number.

## Never (everything Soumil rejected; each line cost a round)
- **Never paste.** No pack cut-out, no code-drawn headline over a generated scene, no die-cut tile, no sticker look as the first version ("it looks like you've just pasted the product and text on pic", Daigo round 2). `scripts/render_ad.py` is only an edit tool for a finished picture (a price swap, a resize, long text).
- **Never draw a product form plain, square or unbranded** ("they look like condom packs"). Always pass its reference; sachets are slim stick packs with the brand print.
- **Never keep a banned claim, badge, seal or icon on the pack.** Use the cleaned pack reference.
- **Never write lines from the claims-safe list.** Voice first, then filter ("really shit text and copywriting", round 1).
- **Never one language only for a bilingual market** ("are you going only with Hinglish?", round 3). Both languages in the pool, a mix in the set.
- **Never ship a line without the native-reader slang screen, and never the trio phaado, daalo, hilao** ("very controversial", round 3). Full list: `references/hinglish-never-use.md`.
- **Never let headline, picture and product tell different stories** ("no harmony", round 2). A literal picture of the words is the weakest kind.
- **Never show an image before the lines are approved as text, and never deliver an unchecked picture** (spelling at zoom, hands, background text, product forms, claim icons).
- **Never assume his taste.** "Real people" and "candid UGC" were guesses the data did not support (people in 40% of his approved ads against 38% of the rejected).
- **Never state a price or offer you did not verify on the live site today; never generate without checking the key and the credit; never take an API key in chat; never upload without his yes** (paused, UTMs, Dropbox, the AI-generated-content declaration Meta asks for at upload).


**Hard rule: verify every offer on the live website before it appears in any ad, every single time.** Before writing copy, and again before delivering, open the brand's live site in a real browser (the homepage top banner, announcement bar, popups, cart, product pages, shipping and returns pages) and confirm each offer, price, discount, shipping term, deadline, review count and guarantee you plan to mention. Never reuse an offer from memory, an old ad, an old page, a search snippet, a competitor read or an earlier round: offers change (Lotus Sculpture, 2026-10-01: the banner had changed from "free shipping" to "40% off shipping" and three rounds of copy were already built on the old wording). Log each verified offer in the vault note with the page it came from and the date. If an offer cannot be verified, leave it out and ask Soumil. Re-check again when regenerating or uploading, because offers can change mid-project.

## Stage 0: Intake
Ask only for what is missing. Required: brand + site URL. Then:
- Meta ad account ID (never assume it; confirm). Past data drives everything, so ask if none.
- Funnel stage: top (cold, problem-led), bottom (warm, offer-led), or both. Default both.
- **Category familiarity** (ask, or decide and state it in the vault note with your evidence): would a random 25-year-old in the target market recognise what this product is from its name alone? Known (toothpaste, deo), known under another name, or NEW (e.g. body glue in India). Known-under-another-name and NEW switch on the explain set in Stage 3. Never assume the category is known because Amazon or the Ad Library is crowded: those show buyers and advertisers, not cold viewers.
- Fixed constraints: client-mandated offer/deal (e.g. Habbits: every ad carries Buy 2 Get 1 Free), claims that must not be used, claims the brand has used before (reusable).
- Products in scope, Shopify/analytics access (optional), how many pictures (default 12 to 15, one picture per concept; both languages are offered for every concept at Gate 2).
- **Data tools and spend (v2.1, 2026-10-09):** run `python scripts/apify_status.py` (read-only) to see the Apify plan, billing cycle, usage and the monthly usage limit. Soumil's rule: never raise the limit without his word; every pull must fit the room left. Ask him once per brand for a yes on each scraped source (Reddit, Amazon, Instagram, Flipkart; public data, none of his logins used). DataForSEO is not bought; buy it only when a brand needs exact search volumes. Amazon no longer needs his Chrome profile (Apify pulls it logged out); the Chrome route is only the fallback. Image generation does not need a browser: it goes through the OpenAI API (Stage 6). Whole-run spend, all on the same OpenAI key: counting about $2 to 3, competitor coding about $0.5, a round of 13 to 15 pictures about $1, the self-check about $0.05; the image ledger sees only pictures, so check the OpenAI billing page before a big research run.

## Stage 1: Past data
Detail and code in `references/research-playbook.md`. Summary:
1. Account by month (`get_insights` level account, time_breakdown month, range maximum). Note eras: best period, pauses, restarts. Purchases = `omni_purchase`.
2. Ad level per era, sorted by spend, with ROAS and CTR. Big outputs are saved to a file by the tool: parse with Python (`encoding='utf-8'`).
3. `bulk_get_ad_creatives` with `fields=id,name,body,title,object_type,image_url,thumbnail_url,object_story_spec` for the top ~25 ads. Extract copy, download images, build a contact sheet, LOOK at the images.
4. Age/gender breakdown for the best era vs now. Audience drift (e.g. women dropped from 36% to 17% of spend) is a finding, not noise.
5. Shopify products.json for live prices and variants. Customer data only if access exists; if not, say so and move on (never block).
6. Write findings: hero product, what promise+proof pattern won, what lost, bundle history, claims used, drift, format saturation ("all statics look the same").
Rule: results judged by ROAS and purchases, not CTR alone (a 2.58% CTR ad had 0.82 ROAS).
Verify before quoting any number (Daigo, 2026-10-09): the account total must equal the sum of its campaigns on every day, the ads must sum to the account, a re-pull must match, and split date ranges must add up; the master note's older figures were later found wrong. For a recent creative test use only settled days (attribution lags), compare against the rest of the account in the same days, and say whether a day-1 spike was luck (3 purchases on Rs 144). Use the official Meta connector read-only; the refresh agent in `references/agent-brief-template.md` (task E) does all of this and saves the scripts that rebuild its CSVs.

## Stage 2: Market research
Detail in `references/research-playbook.md`. Sources, all of them:
- Amazon.in reviews: at least 1,500 reviews per brand run, at least 60% of them 1-3 star (the negative reviews are where customers state their real concerns). Deep pull on the client's products and the top 5 competitors, shallower on 8-10 more competitors and the substitutes. Code the critical reviews into themes and COUNT them ("n of N critical reviews") with `scripts/theme_counter.py` (Jev counts, Luna is the second reader, flagged answers go to a second reader, every share carries its interval; question rules in `references/theme-question-rules.md`; run the 100-review blind audit before quoting any share) plus a manual read of the samples (Stage 3b). The old regex `scripts/theme_count.py` stays only for a quick first look: a one-shot regex scored macro-F1 0.73 against 0.82 for Jev. Pull the reviews with `scripts/amazon_pull.py` (Apify, no login: public layer for breadth, star-filtered pages for the critical reviews; proven on Daigo, 748 reviews for about $1; many ratings are star-only, so quote the star histogram for the rating mix and the text for themes). Recipe, costs and dead ends: `references/data-collection-playbook.md` section 5. The Chrome route in `research-playbook.md` section 2 is the fallback only.
- Reddit through Apify (`scripts/reddit_pull.py`, two phases: find threads with strict matching, then pull the comments of the relevant ones within a budget; Daigo: 3,416 threads and 8,911 comments for about $11). The Arctic Shift script `scripts/reddit_research.py` timed out for hours on 7 and 9 Oct and is the fallback only. Reddit is blocked directly (WebFetch, curl, Chrome). Do not ask Soumil to paste threads. Playbook section 4.
- Google (`scripts/google_pull.py`): free autosuggest in English and Hinglish, People Also Ask and related searches through Apify, one Trends chart for seasonality and the state split. Playbook section 3.
- **Parallel research agents (run first, in parallel):** competitors + ASIN list, open forums, claims and rules audit, brand layer and public reviews, Meta account refresh. Brief and task skeletons in `references/agent-brief-template.md`; stalls, known walls and recovery in the playbook section 2. Outputs per agent are listed there; read their digests before writing Stage 2.
- **Substitutes and workarounds (required, any brand):** what do people use TODAY instead of this product, or to solve the same problem? (Savvy: safety pins, fashion/body tape, double-sided tape, stitching or alterations, skipping the outfit. Habbits: whitening strips, lemon, dentist visits.) Read the reviews of the top 3-5 substitutes on Amazon (critical, 3-star, positive) and the Reddit/Quora threads about the workaround. Capture why people use it, why it fails them, the words they use for the PROBLEM (these people often describe it without any category term), and what would make them switch. Detail in `references/research-playbook.md` section 2b.
- **HARD GATE, competitor CREATIVES (Soumil, 2026-10-09: the Daigo first pass judged competitors by ad titles only and he caught it; the LEAD runs this, the agents never do).** Competitor ads, with images, no browser: `scripts/competitor_sweep.py` (Apify). At least 15 brands (20-25 in a crowded category): direct competitors, category leaders, adjacent brands; up to 30 active ads each, sorted by impressions. It downloads the images, splits genuine static / video / catalog, and builds a numbered contact sheet per brand. LOOK at every sheet and tag each ad (bucket 1-7, offer, hook, format) with run length; then report counts and patterns per industry. Run length is the only winner proxy for IN/US ads. Playbook section 4. `ads_library_search` is only for finding page IDs. Then `scripts/creative_code.py` (v2.2) reads every image with gpt-5-mini vision (about $0.002 an ad), counts format, hook, offer, people, setting, text amount, palette and claims, and builds numbered sheets for any cluster (`code`, `count`, `sheet`, `audit`); check 60 ads by eye before quoting counts. **Stage 2 is NOT complete, and Gate 1 is not presented, until this has run on at least 15 brands and at least 250 ads with the sheets viewed, or Soumil has said to skip it.** Ad titles, body copy and the `ads_library_search` connector alone never count.
- Claims/safety research (regulatory or clinical facts that shape what we can say; e.g. charcoal abrasiveness). For food, drink, health and finance brands run the claims audit agent (FSSAI or the category regulator, ASCI decisions, CCPA, Meta policy): every claim on the site, labels, blogs and ads gets DO NOT SAY / CHANGE TO / CAN SAY WITH PROOF / CAN SAY AS IS with the rule cited. Daigo audit: 42 claims, 24 DO NOT SAY (the whole health layer: probiotic, gut, digestion, "ready in 10 seconds"), and the biggest exposure was the brand's own pages, not its ads. The table feeds the claim register in Stage 4b.
- YouTube comments are unreliable (page freezes). Quora is low signal. Treat as optional.
- **Counting pipeline (v2.1):** pools by source (Amazon, Reddit with the thread title in front, forums), theme questions per source (Reddit starts with an on-topic question), `theme_counter.py --workers 10` (Jev counts, Luna second reader), blind audit with three labellers (`audit_make.py`, `audit_label_mini.py`, a fresh Claude agent, Claude main), `audit_score.py`; quote cleared themes with Wilson intervals and everything else as audit key shares (`audit_key_shares.py`), tables via `counts_report.py`. State the evidence for every awareness claim; Amazon reviewers are buyers, not the cold audience.

## Stage 3: Psychology
Framework in `references/psychology-framework.md`. Vocabulary is fixed:
- **Segment** who. **Driver** what they feel. **Offer** what the creative promises (the core promise, NOT a sale or discount). **Angle** the lens/door in. **Format** how it looks. **Hook** the scroll-stopper. Concept = segment x driver x offer x angle.
- If the client fixes a deal (B2G1), the deal is constant and the **offer axis becomes "why want it"** (routine, results take weeks, moment, safe, dentist price, try it, share it).

Work in this order. Each step writes into the vault note.

**3a. ICP card first (Soumil, 2026-10-04).** Start from what the client said in the onboarding call (`Resources/Ad Inspiration/icp-onboarding-questionnaire.md`) and from Soumil's own ICP knowledge. Write the ICP card: primary ICP, secondary ICP, the wrong customer, 3 purchase triggers, 5 customer phrases, 5 objections, awareness, claims allowed. Mark every line SAID (client belief) or VERIFIED (source: orders, reviews, surveys, Meta breakdowns). Where the data contradicts the client, show it at Gate 1.

**3b. Size the pains by counting (regex plus manual review).** Run `scripts/theme_counter.py` (Jev counts, Stage 2; the old regex `scripts/theme_count.py` only for a quick first look) on the Amazon reviews and the Reddit text. Draft the themes from a first read, run, then READ the match samples and the unmatched-critical list, fix false matches (negations, "worth it" vs "not worth it") and add the themes you missed, re-run until the samples are about 90% true matches and the unmatched list holds no new theme. Only then quote percentages: "about 21% of critical reviews say it does not work". Put the theme table (theme, % of critical reviews, Reddit threads and comments) and the final patterns into the vault note. Evidence labels come from the counts: HIGH = about 15%+ of critical reviews, or confirmed by two independent sources; MED = 5-15% with a second source; LOW = under 5% or one source. A segment or offer resting on LOW evidence gets at most one concept and is labelled LOW. Counts feed segment size and offer ranking; never rate evidence by feel.

**3c. Segments: one axis, no overlap.** Choose ONE axis to split people on for the round (default: the main thing holding them back, i.e. their driver or objection; alternatives: the moment, or the stage of the journey). Each person belongs to exactly ONE segment: the one where their strongest driver sits. Name a PRIMARY ICP (at least half of the round's concepts) and a secondary; other segments only if the counts support them. Overlap test: write one typical person per segment; if the same person fits two segments, merge them or re-cut the axis. Each segment carries: driver, main objection, awareness level with evidence (3d), size evidence (counts from 3b, plus its share of orders or spend and conversion from Stage 1 where available), two exact quotes with source. Check delivery: which segments the current creatives fail to reach, and build a share of creatives for under-served, high-converting segments. Worked example of the default axis (Daigo, 2026-10-09, "the first worry that stops a purchase"): taste doubters (primary), trust doubters, format doubters, value doubters, each sized from the counts; the cold viewer who has never seen the product type is a state, not a segment, and is reached by explain-format concepts inside the primary segment. Persona names in the overlap test are illustrative: say so.

**3d. Awareness evidence per segment.** Never assign an awareness level by judgment. For each segment, read how it writes about its own situation and log the level with the proof: (1) describes the problem with no product words means knows the problem, not the fix; (2) names the category and asks which one to buy means knows the category; (3) names brands and compares them means knows products; (4) already owns the product or responds to offers means ready to buy. Log 1-2 exact quotes and a count of which kind of words the segment uses. Back it with the supporting signals: Reddit or Quora threads asking "what is X" or "does X exist" (low awareness), Google autosuggest for the problem phrase vs the category phrase, how many competitor ads explain the category vs assume it (competitor sweep), the client's answer to the stranger question in the onboarding call, and past Meta results of explain-style vs offer-style ads on cold audiences if they exist. The level decides what the headline must do (table in the framework file).

**3e. Offer bank.** 5-7 core promises ranked by counts and results: theme % from 3b, past winners and their conversion, the brand's own directions. Each offer lists its promise, the segments it serves and its evidence.

**3f. Angles.** 3-4 per offer, each a one-line headline idea plus the format that suits it; mark which need data we do not have. Also list guardrails and what must be verified before use (claims, prices, cart rules). If a Stage 2 claims audit exists, write the voice lines FIRST (Stage 4a) and use its safe-lines list only as a FILTER that fixes wording, then map every LIVE ad headline against it (no flagged phrase / reword / pause; Daigo: 5 of 16 live statics carried a DO NOT SAY phrase, 6 needed rewording, and the winner was 76.6 percent of spend). Show that split at Gate 1; pausing or rewording live ads is Soumil's call and is never done unasked.

Recommended round: one offer per creative, told plainly, plus one native slot (a chat, a tape note) and one loud or funny slot, with the primary ICP getting at least half of the concepts.
**Explain set (required when category familiarity is NEW or known-under-another-name):** top of funnel is split in two. (A) **Explain** creatives for viewers who have never heard of the product type: each must say, in clear everyday words in the image itself (the words the viewer would use, no jargon, no wordplay that needs decoding; the headline style and the visuals stay as creative as you like), (1) the category name, (2) what it does, (3) the problem it solves. (B) **Hook** creatives (recognition, humour, curiosity, enemy, mechanism) for problem-aware viewers. Make at least half of top of funnel explain creatives. Hooks that assume the viewer knows the product type are middle-of-funnel: label them that way.
**GATE 1:** show the ICP card (SAID vs VERIFIED), the theme table, the segment table with awareness evidence, and the concept sheet with one extra column, **Cold read**: what a person who has never heard of this product would think the ad is selling, judged from the image alone. If the cold read is wrong or blank for a top-of-funnel concept, rewrite it before the gate. Add a **Claims status** column (safe line / CHANGE TO / blocked until a document arrives) and give each concept a stable N-number that continues across rounds. Where an older brief contradicts the new data (Daigo: July brief said cast men for cold, the October refresh said women convert cheaper), show both and plan to test both. If the client onboarding call has not happened, say the SAID half of the ICP card is thin and list the 12 live questions as an ask, instead of inventing SAID lines. Worked example (Stage 2 and Stage 3 layout): vault `Projects/daigo-creative-research-oct2026.md`. Recommend picks, wait for Soumil's choice or "go ahead".

## Stage 4: Copy
Principles and template in `references/copy-principles.md`. Order of work:

**4a. Write.** Each concept gets a headline (2-6 words, one idea) plus 2 alternates, a small supporting line (and, after his picks, short and long primary text and a Meta headline), labelled with awareness level, headline type, persuasion lever, reason-why and the headline's origin. **Origin is one of two (Soumil, 2026-10-04):** (1) SOURCED: lifted or adapted from a real customer phrase, with the quote and where it is from; (2) PATTERN-DERIVED: written by me after noticing a pattern in the data (e.g. chai stains show up in 22 reviews and 21 Reddit comments), with the pattern and its count named. Pattern-derived headlines may be curiosity lines that make the stranger want to find out what is meant; they are hypotheses for the ad account to test, and Soumil checks that the line signifies what the data says. Not every headline needs a quote. Write 6 to 10 candidate lines per concept in each language and 4 to 6 small supporting lines; the independent critic cuts them to a BEST per language (4d). On-image lines come first and are what Gate 2 approves.
**Headline and picture are ONE story (a rule, not a guideline; Soumil, 2026-10-10: "no harmony in what the text says and then what the image shows and then the product placement").** The stranger reads the line first and then sees the picture; the picture must prove the idea and the product must take part in it. For every concept write the one-line PICTURE IDEA next to the line, read the two as a single message ("line, then picture: what does the stranger think?") and show the picture idea beside the line at Gate 2. A picture that merely repeats the words is the weakest kind; a plain offer ad still needs the picture to show the offer's mechanism (the pile, the count, the glass).
**Language (v3.0, Soumil, 2026-10-10).** A bilingual market gets BOTH languages: write every concept in Hinglish and in English, run about half and half across the set, and show both options per picture in the Gate 2 table. English is written as a witty local 22 year old, not as an agency; Hinglish as people text. His approved Swipe Desk headlines are mostly English and a Hinglish-only round drew the complaint. Single-language markets follow the language the ICP actually reads for this price point and category; ask Soumil when unsure. A language A/B on the same picture is a fair test and costs about $0.07 a picture.
**Voice first, compliance second (Soumil, 2026-10-09, after he rejected all 17 Daigo round 1 creatives for "shit text and copyrighting" that did not fit the vibe).** Do not write headlines from the safe-claims list: lines written that way are correct and flat. Step 1: read how the audience really talks (the sourced customer words from Stage 2, the competitors' best ads, and the approved ads in `Resources/Ad Inspiration/Swipe Taste Profile.md` when it exists), then write the voice lines first, in the audience's own language or languages (Hinglish and English for his Indian audiences; see Language above), a pool per concept, and read each aloud: would a real person say this to a friend? Step 2: run 4b and 4c as a FILTER on those lines and fix the wording while keeping the voice; a line that has to lose its voice to pass is dropped, not diluted. Step 3: show the lines to Soumil as plain text before any image is built. The critic in 4d also scores "would a person say this", not only clarity and compliance.
**Double-meaning screen (Soumil, 2026-10-10, after he ruled out "Phaado. Daalo. Hilao." as very controversial).** The three verbs tear, put in, shake in that order read as a crude innuendo in Hindi slang, and an independent critic had passed it because nobody was screening for it. Every line, sub-line, chat bubble and badge now goes to a SEPARATE native Hindi and Hinglish reader (a fresh agent) before anyone sees it: crude or sexual double meanings, regional slang, abusive rhymes, political, religious or caste borrowings, alcohol or rule-breaking associations, read alone and next to the picture. HIGH and MED flags are rewritten, LOW are noted. Templates and script: `references/templates/` (`sensitivity_template.md`, `critic_template.md`, `pool.example.json`, `copy_facts.example.md`) and `scripts/make_copy_inputs.py`. Never use phaado, daalo, hilao as a trio or in any order.

**4b. Claim register and live-site proof (hard rule).** Every claim, number, offer, price, guarantee, rating, certification, ingredient or "free from" statement in any copy or image idea gets a row: claim | where it appears on the brand's live properties (homepage banner, announcement bar, popups, cart, any product page, shipping and returns, packaging, Amazon listing, certificate or lab report, anywhere the brand exists) | URL or file | verified yes/no | date. If it is not found on any brand property it cannot appear in the copy: remove it, or ask Soumil, who may accept it as a logged risk. "The brand used it before" is not proof by itself. Compute totals and anchors yourself (Habbits: 249 + 448 + 549 = 1,246, pay 997). Re-run the register at regeneration and at upload. If a Stage 2 claims audit exists, start the register from its `claim_by_claim.md`: DO NOT SAY rows never appear, CHANGE TO rows use the safe wording, CAN SAY WITH PROOF rows need the document in the register first.

**4c. Policy pre-check BEFORE any image is generated.** Run `references/policy-checklist.md` (Meta Advertising Standards plus ASCI claim rules) on the copy AND the image concept together, every creative, every row, with the rule cited. Result per creative: PASS, RISK (Soumil decides, one-line exposure) or FAIL (rewrite first). No image is generated for a FAIL. Re-run on the finished image in Stage 7.

**4d. Critique pass (independent second reader).** After writing, a second reader that did NOT write the copy scores it (details and rubric in `references/copy-principles.md`). The author does not mark their own copy. A second, separate reader screens every line for double meanings (see the double-meaning screen in 4a). Rewrite what fails and keep the table in the vault note. Also run the competitor-sound check: compare headlines with the competitor sweep headlines and rewrite any that sound like a competitor's line. **Single-review rule (Soumil, 2026-10-09):** one negative review about the brand never changes a claim, headline or concept, and the critic's brief says so; only counted themes (cleared in the Stage 2 audit) count. One exception to raise, not decide: a single report of illness or an allergic reaction goes to Soumil, because that is a safety question, not a taste one.

**GATE 2 (v3.0):** show the copy table as plain text BEFORE any image: per picture the PICTURE IDEA, one English and one Hinglish line (one line for a single-language market), your pick, the critic's verdict and the native reader's verdict (PASS, or the flag and the rewrite), the claim register rows with proof, the policy result, the language split, what the slang screen caught, the credit and the cost estimate. The critic and the reader are two fresh agents (`scripts/make_copy_inputs.py`, manual step B, about 40 minutes of waiting for 116 lines); lines you wrote yourself after the screen go back to the same reader. Wait for his picks or "go ahead". Never invent claims, stats or testimonials; timelines are "what to expect", never fake customer quotes. Primary text and Meta headlines for the approved lines are written after his picks.

## Stage 5: Concepts and references (v3.0)
The manual for Stages 5 to 8 is `references/whole-ad-recipe.md`: read it before every round. `references/design-rules.md` and `design-systems.md` now describe LOOKS and limits that you write into each concept; they no longer drive a code layer.
- **References (made once per brand right after Gate 1, saved in `C:\cca\<brand>\ref\`, reused every round):** the clean pack reference (the real pack photo, banned badges, seals and icons removed by the model) and a reference for every product form that appears. The prompts and the forms kit (sachet, jar, tube, box, loose product, scoop) are in the manual, step A. Plain or unbranded forms are never drawn; ask the client for a real photo of any form you had to reconstruct.
- **Brand kit** (Stage 0) supplies the three palette hues; hex codes go into every concept. Variety comes from the ground (yellow, green, blue, cream, split, navy, off-white, a scene), the layout pattern and the scene, not from off-kit colours.
- **Config:** copy `references/templates/ads_config.example.json` to `C:\cca\<brand>\<round>\ads_config.json`. One concept per picture in the fixed anatomy (manual section 5), from the pattern library (section 6): `id`, `num`, `lang`, `line`, `type`, `texts`, `body`. Reference ads from the library (`Desktop\Ad Inspiration\Library\<bucket>\<format>\`, the Swipe Taste Profile) set mood and layout; never copy a competitor.
- **Round mix:** both languages (about half each for a bilingual market), at least 3 scene types and 3 layouts in a round of 6 or more, no background family over about 40%, one native slot (a chat, a tape note) and one loud or funny slot; the primary ICP gets at least half the concepts.
- Hierarchy limits that still hold: one idea and one focal cluster, headline at least 3x any other text, at most 3 text elements (headline, small line, pill or burst), everything inside the central 84%.
- Run `python scripts/whole_ad.py validate --config ...` until it prints ok.

## Stage 6: Generate (v3.0: the model draws the WHOLE ad)
`python scripts/whole_ad.py gen --config ...` (5 pictures in parallel, about 40 seconds each, about 3 minutes and $0.9 for 13; run it in the background and wait for the notification). Each prompt = reference roles + the construction brief + the concept + the avoid list, built by the script. The model draws the scene, the real pack, the branded forms, the headline, the small line and the badges in one pass; NOTHING is composited afterwards (no code text layer, no cut-out pack, no tile: that was the pasted look he rejected).
- Model `gpt-image-2.5-sunburst` (flare costs the same, so there is no draft saving), 1024x1280, quality high, through the OpenAI API (no browser). The key is the user variable OPENAI_API_KEY and the scripts read it from the registry first; rotate with `C:\cca\tools\set_openai_key.ps1` (he copies the key and runs it; never take a key in chat). `python scripts/gen_image.py --ledger` shows spend and the estimated balance; reset `C:\cca\_ledger\balance.json` when he states a credit. Check the credit before a big run with one tiny call. Cost: $0.066 a picture, a 15-picture round about $1 with retries.
- Code text (`scripts/render_ad.py`) remains only as an EDIT tool on a finished picture: a price or date swap, a resize, long text. Never as the first look of a brand ad.
- The old ChatGPT-in-Chrome procedure stays in `references/chatgpt-pipeline.md` as a fallback if the API is unavailable.

## Stage 9: Fix loop
Soumil's feedback is usually specific and small. Regenerate with the prompt patched at the cause: the fault is almost always one sentence in the concept or a missing avoid item (`whole_ad.py gen <id> --tag _v2 --config <ads_config.json>`), then set `src` on its delivery entry and re-run `deliver_ads.py`. ## Soumil's taste (learned; evidence-based since 2026-10-10, apply by default)
- **Loved (Daigo round 4, "I love each and every one of the creatives"):** designed posters, one focal cluster on a flat or studio colour field or a built scene, soft studio light, the real pack integrated with believable shadows and condensation, a headline with a treatment, a concrete number or a joke or a contrast or a question, English and Hinglish lines, picture and line telling one story. The gold set is `references/examples/daigo_round4/gold/` (the sheets and the exact prompts) and `references/templates/ads_config.example.json`.
- **His data** (65 approved of 116 swipes, vault `Resources/Ad Inspiration/Approved ads - how they are built.md`): soft studio light 77%, clean finish 83%, flat or studio ground 52%, the picture demonstrates the idea 71% (the rejected are 67% literal), a type effect on the headline 43%, median headline 4 words and 3 text blocks, product about 12% of the canvas, offer as a badge more often than a burst sticker. People appear in 40% of approved and 38% of rejected ads: not a preference. Candid real-scene photos and people holding the product are not preferred.
- Headline dominant, one focal point. He called Habbits round 1 overfilled ("shabby") and loved the Daigo round 4 set (about 25% empty space, 18% product): the cure for "busy" is cutting elements, never shrinking them.
- Copy must be understood instantly. He rejected: comment threads, "Mine. Yours. Free.", "Shaadi season", a receipt with confusing maths, flat literal lines ("10 sachets. 10 glasses of chaas."), a controversial verb trio. He liked: "One tube won't do it", "3 problems, pay for 2", clean receipts with a plain total, "Ticket confirm. Chaas confirm.", "Roommates exist. Get 20.", "Waitlisted? Chaas is confirmed.", "Could've been an email. Chaas couldn't.", the equation poster and the loud shout poster.
- Offer means the core promise, not a discount. Bottom-funnel offer ads must read as "pick ANY 3 from the range", not a fixed pack.
- Clay/diorama styles and generic brand-DNA template output are rejected. Arcads-style templates stay the route for UGC VIDEO only.
- No made-up claims, stats, testimonials or label text.
- Top-of-funnel for an unfamiliar category must explain what the product is, what it does and the problem, in plain words in the image (Soumil, Savvy, 2026-10-01: "I don't know that a product like body glue exists"). Clever or emotional hooks alone are middle-of-funnel. The equation poster ("sachet + water = chaas") is the model explain picture.
- Word counts in the rules are soft guides, never hard caps. Do not cut a word that the idea needs; cut words that do not earn their place (Soumil, 2026-10-01).
- Show creatives for picking with big numbers 1..N on each image plus a number = line key, so he can eliminate by number. Show the exact prompts when he asks ("tell me the exact prompts you gave to the llm").
- Money and keys are his: never top up, never take a key in chat; say what is needed and why, naming the account.
- Rejected in order (each is a Never at the top of this file): round 1 flat code text on scenes and literal lines (9 Oct), round 2 pasted pack and text, plain sachets, no harmony (10 Oct), round 3 Hinglish only and a controversial trio (10 Oct).
