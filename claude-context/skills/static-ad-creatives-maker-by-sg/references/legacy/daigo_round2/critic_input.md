# Independent copy critic: Daigo round 2 headlines (Hinglish)

You did not write these lines. Your job is to judge them like a sharp Indian ad copywriter AND a compliance reader, then pick the best. Work alone. Write your answer to `C:\cca\daigo\round2\critic_output.md` (markdown, the table format at the bottom). Do not browse the web. Do not generate images.

## The brand and the buyer
Daigo sells an instant chaas (spiced buttermilk) mix in single-serve sachets (Masala flavour). You tear a sachet, add cold water, shake, and you have a glass. Buyers: 20 to 30 year olds in Indian metros who are away from home (hostel, PG, first job, trains, road trips, office). They read and speak Hinglish (Hindi in Roman letters mixed with English). The ads are static Meta ads, 1:1, with one big headline (2 to 7 words) over a real-looking scene.

## Why this round exists
The client rejected a first round of 17 ads: "the text is not going with the overall vibe, really shit text and copywriting". Those lines were literal, correct and flat ("10 sachets. 10 glasses of chaas."). He approves ads whose headline is bold, a little funny or exaggerated, and talks straight to the reader. Examples he approved from other brands: "SOOOOOOOO MUCH WATER!!!", "TIRED OF SUGAR CRASH?", "LESS SUGAR. HUGE FLAVOR.", "Want 40g of protein before 9am? This is for you.", "15% OFF", "MAKEUP ARTIST APPROVED NUDES", "Instant Coverage, Instant Magic". He liked his September Daigo ads with Hinglish headlines in capitals: "TRAIN KA KHANA. GHAR KI CHAAS.", "PG KA KHANA. GHAR KI CHAAS.", "ROAD TRIP? CHAAS IS PACKED.", "EK PACKET OPEN KRO. EK GLASS READY." (those used claims that are now banned, see below, but the VOICE is what he liked).

## Facts the lines may use (verified, nothing else)
- Tear, add cold water, shake (the pack directions). No curd to set, no blender or mixer to wash, no mess.
- One sachet makes one glass. 10 sachets = 10 glasses; 20 = 20; 30 = 30. Prices: Rs 279 (10), Rs 529 (20), Rs 749 (30). Per glass: Rs 28, Rs 26, Rs 25.
- Extra 5% off on prepaid orders with code PREPAY5 (20-pack ad only).
- Occasions: train, trip, PG, hostel, office. The feeling "when you miss ghar ki chaas" is allowed as a feeling, never as a taste claim.
- Do not use delivery or free-shipping wording.

## Banned (the brand's claims audit says DO NOT SAY). Any line containing or implying these fails
real, fresh, natural, authentic, pure, genuine, "ghar jaisa" / "just like home" / "same taste as homemade" (any taste-equivalence), probiotic, gut, digestion, acidity, bloating, calcium, protein, healthy, health, superfood, guilt-free, low calorie, energy, slump (3 PM slump), focus, hydrate, electrolytes, cooling, "heat" relief, first / pioneer / only, any seconds or minutes ("10 seconds", "1 minute", "in no time" is also risky), "zero preservatives", "no preservatives", "no fridge needed", comparisons with other products (tetra pack, packaged buttermilk, other brands), vrat / fasting / Jain / Navratri, "limited time", countdowns, any customer quote or review, any health or weight claim, any claim about taste quality ("tastes great", "best chaas"). Also avoid insulting the reader and anything about alcohol, religion, caste, region stereotypes or breaking hostel rules.

## What to do
For each concept below, the candidate lines are numbered. Do this for every concept:
1. Score each candidate 1 to 5 on: (a) would a real 22 year old say or share this, (b) Hinglish that reads natural, not translated or forced, (c) stops the scroll at 4 words or fewer when read on a phone, (d) one idea only.
2. Mark any candidate that breaks the banned list or the facts list: write FAIL and the rule it breaks. Be strict: an implied claim counts.
3. Pick BEST and ALT (the best two that are not FAIL). If none of the candidates is good enough, write up to 3 better lines of your own (also checked against the banned list).
4. One sentence on why BEST wins.
Then at the end: list the 6 strongest lines across all concepts in order, and say which two concepts are weakest and should be cut.
