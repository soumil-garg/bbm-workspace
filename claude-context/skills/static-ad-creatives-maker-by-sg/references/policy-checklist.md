# Policy pre-check (Stage 4c): run on the copy AND the image concept BEFORE any image is generated

Why: a rejected or restricted ad wastes the whole generation round and can put the client's ad account at risk. Check the idea, not just the finished picture. Re-run on the finished image in Stage 7.

Sources (re-read when in doubt, policies change): Meta Advertising Standards, fetched 2026-10-04: transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/ (Privacy Violations and Personal Attributes; Health and Wellness; Prohibited Commercial Practices). Secondary 2026 summaries say enforcement now reads text, image, landing page and comments together and also catches indirect phrasing. India: ASCI (Advertising Standards Council of India) code, health and personal care is its most-complained category and claims must be substantiated. This checklist is my reading of those rules for ad work, not legal advice. For anything borderline, mark RISK and ask Soumil; for a health or regulated claim ask the client for their substantiation (lab report, certificate, study).

## Checklist (answer every row for every creative; cite the rule)
1. **Personal attributes.** No copy or image that asserts or implies the viewer's health, body, appearance, medical condition, age, etc. No "you/your" tied to such an attribute ("Your teeth are yellow", "Are you a diabetic?"). Write about the product, the habit or the moment, or in first person ("I zoomed into the group photo"). Check conditional phrasing ("If you struggle with...") and empathy hooks ("We understand your pain").
2. **Negative self-perception.** No statements of inferiority about physical appearance or hygiene, no questions that attack appearance, no fear-based pressure on insecurities, used to sell a health, wellness or cosmetic product (Meta Health and Wellness rule). Stains and chai are fine; "ugly yellow teeth" is not.
3. **Unrealistic outcomes.** No miracle, guaranteed or sensational claims; no promise of a specific result within a set timeframe without a qualifier or disclaimer (Meta). "3X whiter in 30 days" and "results in 7 days" fail unless qualified and substantiated. Safer: what to expect, with honest qualifiers ("most people see a change in about 4 weeks of regular use").
4. **Before and after.** Not for health, weight-loss or cosmetic-outcome claims (strict for weight loss and procedures); avoid side-by-side transformation imagery on products that promise a body or appearance result. A teeth-whitening before/after is a risk: mark RISK and avoid unless Soumil decides otherwise.
5. **Body-focused imagery.** No close-ups that single out a body area in a way that shames it (pinching fat, zoomed blemish or stain shots as a negative); no unrealistic body imagery.
6. **Deceptive commercial practices.** Prices, "free", "buy 2 get 1", discounts, deadlines and urgency must be real and match the live site and the cart (Prohibited Commercial Practices). No fake countdowns, no strikethrough prices that were never charged, no unauthorised endorsements, no fake or composite testimonials presented as real.
7. **Claim substantiation (Meta + ASCI).** Every factual claim (numbers, "clinically proven", "dentist recommended", "peroxide-free", "10,000+ customers", ratings, awards) must be in the claim register with a source found on the brand's site, pack, product listing, certificate or test report. Superlatives and comparisons ("best", "No.1", "better than X") need proof and a named basis. No disparaging a named competitor.
8. **Health and regulated product claims.** No diagnosing, treating, curing or preventing claims for conditions unless the product is licensed for it; Drugs and Magic Remedies rules apply in India. Cosmetic effects only, in cosmetic language.
9. **Landing-page consistency.** The page must carry the same offer and claims, and must not escalate beyond the ad. Check the live page for the same offer, price and claim.
10. **Text and image together.** The image can create an implied claim the words do not (a person with perfect teeth next to "3 weeks"). Read headline, image idea and sub-line as one message and re-run rows 1-5 on the combination.
11. **Sensitive imagery.** Nudity or suggestive posing, minors, medical imagery that shocks, weapons, tobacco use. Check people shots (modest clothing, anatomy).
12. **Age and audience.** Cosmetic and dietary product ads must be targeted at 18+ in Meta's rules; teeth whitening is exempt from the age restriction per Meta's page, but check the current wording before launch.
13. **Double meaning, slang and sensitivity (added 2026-10-10).** Every line, sub-line, chat bubble and badge is read by a native Hindi and Hinglish reader (a separate agent) for crude or sexual double meanings, regional slang, abusive rhymes, political or religious borrowings and alcohol or rule-breaking associations, in the line alone and next to its picture. Known ban: the verb trio phaado, daalo, hilao (tear, put in, shake) in any order or part, ruled out by Soumil as very controversial. FAIL until rewritten; keep the screen output in the vault note. The reader's full list of screened-out words, combinations and picture risks: `references/hinglish-never-use.md`.
14. **Supplements and health-adjacent products (added 2026-10-10; a guideline, not legal advice).** Copy says what the product is and how to use it, never what it treats or prevents; use only claims that the brand's licensed label and the Stage 2 claims audit allow (the regulator of the market: FSSAI in India; FTC and FDA rules elsewhere); mandatory disclaimers come from the client's compliance, never from guesswork; when no claims audit exists, run one BEFORE copy and before making the clean pack reference, and compare the pack photo with the claim register (4b).

## Output (vault note, per creative)
| ID | Rule row | Finding | PASS / RISK / FAIL | Rewrite |
PASS = ship. RISK = Soumil decides (state the exposure in one line). FAIL = rewrite before any image is generated. Do not generate an image for a FAIL.
