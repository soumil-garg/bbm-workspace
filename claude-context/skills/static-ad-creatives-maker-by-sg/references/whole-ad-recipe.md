His data-backed taste (65 approved of 116 swipes, vault `Resources/Ad Inspiration/Approved ads - how they are built.md`): designed, studio-lit posters on a flat or built colour field, a hero cluster with a believable shadow, a headline with a typographic treatment, the picture demonstrating the idea (65 to 71% of approved against 29 to 33% of rejected; the rejected are 67% literal), a concrete number, joke, contrast or question. Not preferred: candid UGC, people holding the product, a vague mood line.

## 2. The output standard (every run delivers exactly this)
- 4:5 pictures at 1024x1280 from the model, delivered as 1080x1350 PNG, numbered 1..N, clean file names, one concept per picture.
- Poster look: one focal cluster, a flat colour field or a built studio set or a believable scene, one soft key light with specular highlights and condensation, three-level type (a huge headline of 2 to 7 words on two lines (three stacked lines only when the middle one is a huge number, as in UNDER / RS 30 / A GLASS.), an optional small supporting line, an optional pill or burst), a treatment on the headline, a palette of at most three brand hue families plus white and the product's own colours (their tints, shades and neutrals are fine; the Daigo set used seven hex values), generous margins (everything inside the central 84%).
- The real pack and every product form drawn from reference images: correct, branded, lit by the scene, physically in it.
- Headline, picture and product are one story. The line says the idea, the picture proves it, the product takes part in it.
- Both languages for a bilingual market: about half and half in the set, every concept written in both.
- Every line passed the independent critic, the claim register, the policy checklist and the native-reader slang screen, and Soumil approved the lines as text before any image existed.
- Delivered with numbered contact sheets (4 columns, up to 8 a sheet), `PROMPTS_roundN_exact.md` (word for word), the folder opened, the sheets and prompts sent to him.
- Cost about $0.066 a picture, a round of 13 to 15 about $1 with retries; the ledger reset to the credit he states.
- Then the message: the key, the cost, what was checked, the self-check gap, what is not clean, your five picks and why, what is not done, ask for picks by number.

## 3. Step by step
### Step A. Setup (once per brand, then reuse)
1. Key and credit: `python scripts/gen_image.py --ledger`. The key lives only in the user variable OPENAI_API_KEY; rotate with `C:\cca\tools\set_openai_key.ps1` (he copies the key, runs it; never take a key in chat). Spend test: `python scripts/gen_image.py --check` (one gpt-5-mini call, about $0.00002; HTTP 429 insufficient_quota means no credit; say which account needs the top-up, topping up is his action). The ledger is an estimate: set `start_usd` and `as_of` (ISO datetime) in `C:\cca\_ledger\balance.json` when he states the credit.
2. Clean pack reference. Take the best real pack photo (the brand's own site gallery, white background). If the claims audit bans anything printed on it (a badge, a seal, an icon row, a claim), have the model remove exactly that and nothing else. The Daigo prompt, the model edited `masala_01.jpg` with it ($0.06):
   "Edit this product photograph of a stand-up pouch. Remove exactly three things and change nothing else: (1) the small white circular badge with the "10's" text inside the green tab at the top right of the blue panel: leave the green tab plain and smooth; (2) the white scalloped round seal with small text on the left side of the blue panel, below "Classic Masala": fill that area with the same blue background and faint pattern around it; (3) the row of four round icons with their small captions in the lower green band: fill that area with the plain green of the band. Everything else must stay identical: [list every element to keep: shape, logo, name, flavour line, illustration, bowl, shadow, white background]. Do not add any text, icon, badge or claim anywhere."
   Call: `python scripts/gen_image.py --prompt-file p.txt --ref <photo> --out pack_clean.png --size 1024x1024 --tag <brand>_pack_clean`. Check the result against the photo at zoom (spelling of every kept word; nothing added).
3. Product-form reference for every form that appears in ads (sachet, stick pack, jar, tube, box). If the brand has no real photo of the form, make one from the clean pack. The Daigo prompt for the sachet: "Studio product photograph on a pure white seamless background: two slim stick-pack sachets of the same Daigo chaas mix shown from the front, standing upright side by side, slightly angled, soft contact shadows. Each sachet is a slim vertical foil stick pack about 3.5 cm wide and 13 cm tall with fine crimped (zig-zag) silver foil seals at the top and bottom. The front panel is printed in the same royal blue as the pouch in the reference image, carrying the white italic "daigo" wordmark with the small line "dairy on the go" under it near the top (copied from the pouch in the reference image), then the words "Instant Chaas Premix" in green and a small green stripe at the bottom; no other text, no icons, no badges, no claims. The sachets must look like branded consumer packaging, clearly printed, premium and real, never plain, never square, never like a small square packet. Realistic metallic foil texture on the crimped edges, crisp print, natural studio lighting." Ask the client for a photo of the real form and replace the reference when it arrives (Daigo's sachet front is still a reconstruction).
   Plain or square unbranded forms read as condom packs. Never describe a form as plain, never omit its reference.
4. Folders: `C:\cca\<brand>\ref\` holds the references (clean pack, one per form, logos) for every round; each round has `C:\cca\<brand>\<round>\` with `ads_config.json`, `copy\` (the copy gate), `p\` (prompts), `out\` (pictures), `qa\`. Every command below is `python <skill>/scripts/<name>.py ...` with `<skill>` = `C:/Users/soumi/.claude/skills/static-ad-creatives-maker-by-sg` (forward slashes in Git Bash); the recipe writes `python scripts/<name>.py` for short.

### Step A2. Forms kit (a jar, a tube, a box, loose product, a scoop; the Daigo pouch and sachet are only the worked example)
- Same two-reference shape for every brand: image 1 = the clean pack front, image 2 = a reference of each other form that appears. Never more than 3 or 4 references in one call (fidelity drops).
- Jar or bottle: front-label crop on a white ground, lid colour and shape named, which side faces the camera ("label facing us, lid on"). ref_rules (b): "The jar has a <colour> screw-top lid and a wrap-around label printed exactly as in the reference; the label always faces the viewer; never an unlabelled jar."
- Tube: crimped end at the top or bottom stated, cap down or up stated, the printed front facing us. Box or carton: the front panel and one side.
- Loose product (gummies, capsules, tablets, powder) carries no print, so the "never plain" rule moves to the container: show it in or beside the real pack, never as a hero on its own; describe colour and shape only as the reference shows; never invent a pill, gummy or capsule the brand does not sell. A scoop of powder: say "a level scoop", pale colour, no cloud (use the neg_preset "powder").
- Dense label text (supplement facts, ingredients, barcode, small print) is re-drawn by the edits endpoint and may garble: crop the FRONT panel only, list the words to keep in the reference prompt, compare every word at zoom, and if the small print garbles ask the client for a clean packshot instead of shipping a garbled label. QA check C requires every printed word spelled right.
- No white-ground photo on the site: photograph or ask for one; a lifestyle photo with props leaks the props into the ads.
- A pack nobody has audited: an unaudited printed claim would be reproduced in every ad. Run the claims audit (Stage 2) BEFORE making the clean pack reference, and compare the pack photo with the claim register (4b).
- A real photo of a form that the brand has but you only reconstruct (Daigo sachet front): ship the reconstruction for ONE round at most, say so in the message, and ask the client for the photo.


### Step B. The copy gate (lines first, about 40 minutes of waiting)
1. Write the pool: for every picture concept, 6 to 10 candidate lines, about half in each language (English written as a witty local 22 year old, not as an agency; Hinglish as people text). Also write 4 to 6 candidate supporting lines. Calibrate on his approved headline patterns: contrast ("X. Not Y."), exaggeration, a joke, a direct question, a concrete number or price, a mild put-down of an annoying situation. Never on the claims-safe list.
2. Write `copy_facts.md` (blurb, why this round exists, verified facts, banned list; format `references/templates/copy_facts.example.md`) and `pool.json` (format `references/templates/pool.example.json`).
3. `python scripts/make_copy_inputs.py --pool pool.json --facts copy_facts.md --out <round>\copy --brand <Brand> --round <N> --swipe`.
4. Spawn two fresh general-purpose agents in the background, in parallel, neither the author of the lines: the critic (`critic_input.md` to `critic_output.md`) and the native Hindi and Hinglish reader (`sensitivity_input.md` to `sensitivity_output.md`). Daigo: 116 lines took about 36 minutes for both. While they run, do the other housekeeping; do not poll.
5. Reconcile: the critic's BEST per language and its recommended set, minus every line the reader flagged MED or HIGH, plus the reader's safe rewrites. Lines you wrote yourself after the screen go back to the same reader (SendMessage to its agent id, about 2 minutes).
6. Gate 2 as plain text: a table per picture with one English and one Hinglish line, the pick, and the screen result; the language split; what the screen caught and changed; the credit and the cost estimate; ask for picks. Wait for him. Do not generate before the "go".

### Step C. Build the round
1. Copy `references/templates/ads_config.skeleton.json` (blank; validate refuses it until every placeholder is filled) to `<round>\ads_config.json` and read `ads_config.example.json` (the Daigo gold config) beside it. Fill: `brand`, `round`, `dir`, `dest`, `tag_prefix`, `palette` (three hue families from the brand kit; tints, shades and neutrals of them are fine), `finish` ("specular highlights and condensation" for a drink, "specular highlights" for dry goods), `small_line`, `refs` (path and role sentence for each reference), `ref_rules` (four sentences, section 4), `neg_preset` (["drink"], ["powder"] or []) with `neg_extra` (or `neg_items` to replace the whole avoid list), `required_texts` (a client-mandated deal that must be on every poster), `offers_verified` (offer, page, date: checked on the live site today), `languages`, `banned_phrases` (the claim bans as regular expressions, applied to every on-image text; in JSON write `\\b` for a word boundary), `concepts`, `delivery`.
2. Each concept: `id`, `num`, `lang`, `line`, `type` (poster or native), `texts` (every exact on-image string), `body` written in the anatomy of section 5 with `{TOP}` and `{SMALL}`. Pick the nearest pattern from section 6 and adapt; do not invent a new layout style unless the idea needs it.
3. `python scripts/whole_ad.py validate --config ...` until it says ok (reference files exist, every exact text is in the prompt, posters carry a HEADLINE and the top-margin rule, no banned slang word or claim in an on-image text, language mix printed).
4. `python scripts/whole_ad.py gen --config ...` (5 in parallel, about 40 seconds each, about 3 minutes for 13). Run it in the background and wait for the notification. It never overwrites a picture that exists (`--force` or a new `--tag` such as `_v2`), and a tagged run writes its own prompt file `p/<id>_v2.txt`.
5. QA (section 9), regenerate fails with `python scripts/whole_ad.py gen <id> --tag _v2 --config <ads_config.json>` after patching the sentence that caused the fault (at most 2 regenerations per picture; a third failure means the concept or the reference is wrong), then deliver (section 11).

### Step D. After his picks
Alternate-language versions of a picture (a clean language test, about $0.07 each), 9:16 versions of the winners (the model has no 9:16 size; the nearest is 1024x1536, which is 2:3 and delivers at 1080x1620, so Meta will pad or crop it for Stories; keep the headline first line at least 14% below the top and everything in the central 84%; UNTESTED, generate one and look before doing the rest), Meta primary texts and headlines for the approved lines, then upload (ask first; paused, UTMs `utm_campaign={{campaign.name}}&utm_content={{ad.name}}`, Dropbox, AI declaration).

## 4. The prompt: four blocks, joined by single spaces (built by `whole_ad.py`)
1. ROLE_REFS: "REFERENCE IMAGES: image 1 is <role of reference 1>; image 2 is <role of reference 2>." followed by four sentences you author per brand, keeping their shape:
   (a) "Reproduce the <pack> and the <forms> faithfully from these references: <every visible feature to keep: colours, wordmark and tagline, product name, flavour, the illustration, the bowl>."
   (b) the form rule: "<Forms> are SLIM VERTICAL STICK PACKS with crimped silver foil ends and the same blue printed front with the <brand> wordmark: never plain, never square, never a small square packet." (adapt the shape words to the form)
   (c) "Do not add any badge, seal, icon, claim or extra wording to the <pack> or the <forms> beyond what the references show."
   (d) "<Pack> and <forms> are sharp, large enough to read, lit by the same light as the scene, with contact shadows and believable perspective, physically part of the scene (standing, leaning, held or resting on surfaces), never floating pasted-on cut-outs."
2. ART (constant; only the palette and, for dry goods, the finish words change ("specular highlights" without "and condensation", config field `finish`); do not shorten it): "Create a FINISHED professional Meta feed advertisement, 4:5 portrait, art directed like a top D2C brand poster, in the construction style of the best static ads: ONE clear focal point; a disciplined palette of at most three brand hues (<palette>) plus white and the product's own colours; the background is a designed colour field or a built studio set, not a casual snapshot; clean soft studio lighting with soft believable shadows, specular highlights and condensation; three-level type hierarchy (a huge headline, one small supporting line, optional tiny badge); the typography is part of the design (it overlaps, wraps or sits behind elements where that helps) and carries one treatment such as a highlight bar, an underline swoosh or a two-colour split; the headline and the picture tell ONE story and the product takes part in that story; generous margins, all text and key objects inside the central 84% of the frame, nothing important touching an edge. Every piece of text must be spelled EXACTLY as given, letter for letter, cleanly rendered and aligned. No other text, no watermark, no fake UI."
3. The concept body (section 5).
4. NEG: "Avoid: a pasted-on look, flat cut-out products, a casual phone-snapshot look, empty unused space, plain unbranded product forms, stock-photo smiles, extra logos, invented claims, health claims, any text that was not specified, garbled lettering, extra fingers, readable text on any background object (book spines, signboards, labels, screens) other than the text specified, white powder clouds, white liquid splashes or sprays, clinking glasses, unlabelled clear bottles, the logo of any real company." plus brand items (the form's shape, "sachets tucked into pockets", "the logo of the railways" when a ticket appears).

## 5. The concept body (the anatomy; every sentence has a job)
Poster: "CONCEPT: <the idea in one sentence> as a <clean | bold | studio hero> poster. Background: <flat colour field (#hex) with a faint texture | studio sweep | scene>. HEADLINE <at the top | across the upper third ABOVE his head>, huge, all caps, heavy condensed grotesque sans-serif, tight tracking: <LINE 1> in <colour> on the first line and <LINE 2> in <colour> on the second line, <the treatment>. {TOP} HERO <where>: <every object named with its state>; <where the real pack stands>; <light>. <Badge or burst with its exact text>. Small supporting line at the bottom in navy: {SMALL} No other text."
Native (a chat, a tape note, a still life): "CONCEPT: ... The chat (or the tape note) is the headline; there is no other text." Quote every bubble in order and the exact count: "exactly three message bubbles and nothing else (no names, no avatars, no status icons, no keyboard)".
Rules that came from failures: the first headline line starts at least 9% below the top; with a person, the whole headline sits ABOVE the head and the person stands lower (a head covered the word MAT once); list every exact string; capitals for handwriting ("every letter a capital letter and no lowercase letter anywhere"; a lowercase i slipped through once); straight apostrophes; two headline lines (three stacked only when the middle one is a huge number); "no book spines, titles or labels anywhere" and "no signboards" for anything scenic (a DHABA sign and book spines appeared); name how each product form faces the camera ("printed blue fronts facing us"); hands "with five fingers each"; "nobody in the car", "parked".

## 7. Picture-safety rules (the slang screen's list; they cost nothing)
No sachet or packet in a pocket, handed out of a bag or pulled from one (a stash reading); no clinking glasses (a toast); no unlabelled clear bottle in a car (use the shaker); no white powder cloud or white splash (show the pale drink poured into a steel tumbler; a pile of forms stacked neatly with the print visible); a fictional ticket with no railway logo, PNR or train number; nobody sitting on a ledge; lanyards and laptops without names; a vegetarian thali; no real-company logos; handwriting that looks playful.

## 8. Language and lines
- Both languages for a bilingual market, about half and half; English as a witty local 22 year old, Hinglish as people text; keep one idea; 2 to 5 words is best.
- Never the trio phaado, daalo, hilao; never a line the native reader flags MED or HIGH; the full list: `references/hinglish-never-use.md`.
- The small supporting line defines the product when the headline does not (Daigo: "Masala chaas mix. One sachet, one glass."); never the pack's directions as a verb string.
- Verify every price and offer on the live site the day you build; compute totals yourself ("Under Rs 30 a glass" is true because 279 / 10 = 27.9).
- Claims: the register (4b) and the policy checklist (4c) run as filters AFTER the voice lines are written, never as the writer.

## 9. QA before he sees anything (about 10 minutes; `references/qa-gate.md` has the full list)
1. `python scripts/whole_ad.py qa --config ...` builds 3-column sheets in `<round>\qa\`; view them, then crop and zoom on every headline, small line, badge, chat bubble, tape note, hand and pack print.
2. Tick off every string in each concept's `texts` letter by letter (apostrophes, commas, full stops, the number of O's and exclamation marks).
3. The pasted test, the harmony test, the product-form test, the claim-icon test, background text, hands, the slang screen done, the language split (qa-gate checks A to H).
4. Self-check against his approved ads: `python C:\cca\inspiration\swipe\construction_selfcheck.py <label> <pictures...>` (about $0.003 a picture); compare with the approved row: clean finish 83%, soft studio light 77%, flat or studio ground 52%, the picture demonstrates the idea 71%, a type effect 43%, product 12% of the canvas, empty space 40%. Daigo round 4: clean finish 87%, demonstrates 73%, type effect 87%, but empty space 25% and product 18%: fuller than his approved ads, yet he loved it; report the gap and offer to strip back.
5. Regenerate a fail with the sentence that caused it patched. Typical faults and fixes: a head covering a headline word (headline above the head, person lower); background text (add "no readable text on background objects"); a lowercase letter in handwriting (capitals sentence); plain square forms (the form reference and the form rule); white powder (pale drink, tidy stack); the model ignoring "top 30% empty" (use a position instruction, not an emptiness one).

## 10. Cost, speed, limits
$0.066 a 1024x1280 picture (the budget of a whole run, research included, is in SKILL.md Stage 0; at most 2 regenerations per picture) (text in $5, image in $8, image out $30 per million tokens; flare costs the same, so use sunburst); 5 in parallel, about 40 seconds each; 13 pictures about 3 minutes and $0.86; retries about 30%. ## 12. Removed from v2 (and why it must not come back)
- The code text layer as the default (`render_ad.py` text splits by bucket, pack cut-outs, die-cut tiles, light pools behind a pasted pack, the measured product-separation check as the main check): it produced the pasted look (round 2).
- Plain or unbranded product forms in scene prompts; "empty corners kept for the pack"; scenes written without the headline.
- One language only; lines written from the claims-safe list; a critic without a slang screen.
- The belief that real people or candid photos are his taste (the data says no); people only when the idea needs them.
- Showing images before the lines are approved.
- Hard "at least 40% empty space" as a rule: the loved set sits at 25%; the rule is one focal cluster and a headline first.
