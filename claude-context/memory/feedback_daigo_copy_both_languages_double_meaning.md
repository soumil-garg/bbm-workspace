On 10 Oct 2026, after round 3: "the graphic designs are really, really better than before ... Are you going only with Hinglish ones here? The statement phaado daalo hilao is a very controversial statement. Don't use that. I want you to come up with better copywriting. We can go with some of the Hinglish ones, but we'll also have to use some English ones as well."

**Why:** the trio phaado, daalo, hilao (tear, put in, shake) reads as crude innuendo in Hindi slang. I had put it in as a candidate line myself and the independent critic passed it, because neither of us screened for double meanings. It shipped into numbers 1, 2, 4, 5 and 12 of round 3. His approved Swipe Desk headlines are mostly English (contrast, exaggeration, jokes, a question, a concrete number), so a Hinglish-only set was also the wrong default.

**How to apply:**
1. Never use phaado, daalo or hilao as a trio or in any order; for the how-to line use the pack directions in English ("Tear. Add water. Shake.") or other verbs.
2. Every line, sub-line, chat bubble and badge goes to a SEPARATE native Hindi and Hinglish reader (fresh agent) before he sees it; HIGH and MED flags are rewritten. Templates in the skill `references/examples/daigo_round4/` and policy-checklist row 13; the reader's full never-use list is `references/hinglish-never-use.md` (e.g. "Bhai, chaas hai? Haan. Bag mein." reads as a drug-ask script, "kam padenge" reads as a condom-pack line, "haath lagana" can mean hit or grope).
3. Write each concept in both Hinglish and English and show both options per picture; run about half and half; a language A/B on the same picture is a fair test.
4. Calibrate copy on his approved headline patterns ([[feedback-whole-ad-generation-harmony]], vault Approved ads - how they are built): contrast ("X. Not Y."), exaggeration, a joke, a direct question, a concrete number.
5. Check the claims list and the double-meaning screen before showing lines as text; he approves lines before images.
