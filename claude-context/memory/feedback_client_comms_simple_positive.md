Client-side messages (WhatsApp, reports) use plain everyday words a non-specialist reads at once, and lean positive: problems go in as opportunities or quick wins, not as criticism. Said by Soumil on 2026-10-09 when I wrote "21 percent with meaning and meditation" in the Kyle conclusions text: "things should be really simple to read and mostly on the positive side, no negative shit".

**Why:** Kyle is a non-specialist owner; Soumil wants him to feel steady progress and a positive effect (also his earlier rule for the Lotus group: show we are always working, always a positive impact). Pairs with [[feedback-solo-voice-first-person]] and [[feedback_tone]].

**How to apply:** before drafting, replace jargon and category labels with what a person would say ("1 in 8 buy for prayer" not "12 percent devotional, 21 with meaning and meditation"); say what is good first and what the ads can do next; no "8 of 15 ads break the rules", say "a few small wording changes will make the ads stronger"; keep counts that impress (items read) and drop counts that sound like blame; "I", never "we"; short, no asterisks, no em-dashes. Keep the full unflattering detail in internal notes and send it only when Soumil asks.

**Added 2026-10-10 (Soumil, Lotus):** never put promises or process details about customers in a message to the client, for example "I will check with each customer before their video goes live". He was angry I wrote it into the Kyle message ("dont fucking say that"). Keep client messages to what was made, the link, and the one ask. Handle customer permissions quietly on our side.
